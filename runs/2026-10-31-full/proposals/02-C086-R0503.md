## Post-Quantum Firmware Architecture for the KL-HSM Line: Integrating ML-DSA into a Resource-Constrained ARM Cortex-M HSM

**Company:** Kriptolog a.d., Novi Sad (Cybersecurity) [C086]
**Faculty:** Faculty of Technical Sciences, University of Novi Sad — Department of Computing and Control Engineering
**Academic supervisor:** Milica Nikolić (Milica Nikolić), Full Professor [R0503]
**Industrial supervisor:** to be named by Kriptolog — the company's lead cryptographic firmware engineer (the role advertised in February 2024 [C086-P2] is the natural candidate if filled)
**Candidate:** to be recruited from Kriptolog's engineering staff or via open call; must hold an engineering degree and have embedded C experience
**Start:** January 2027

---

### The problem

Kriptolog's KL-HSM line currently performs RSA and ECDSA signing operations. Two national-level agencies and one payment network operator have asked in writing whether those operations will remain secure once capable quantum hardware exists [C086-CH1]. Kriptolog has had one engineer prototype ML-DSA (formerly Dilithium) but has not changed the firmware architecture to handle the larger key material or different signature flow that ML-DSA requires. The KL-500 runs custom ARM Cortex-M7 firmware in C [C086], and a straight drop-in of ML-DSA is not possible given flash and RAM constraints [C086-CH1]. Kriptolog needs a documented migration path that satisfies procurement officers planning five- to ten-year infrastructure cycles and that can survive a Common Criteria evaluation at EAL4+ [C086] [C086-P2].

---

### Research questions

1. **Memory and performance fit:** Which ML-DSA parameter set (ML-DSA-44, -65, or -87) can be implemented on the KL-500's ARM Cortex-M7 within the flash and RAM envelope of the existing firmware, and what is the signing latency in milliseconds under that constraint? Success criterion: a working implementation with measured signing time and peak RAM use reported against the current ECDSA baseline.

2. **Hybrid operation:** What firmware architecture allows the KL-HSM to sign with both ECDSA and ML-DSA simultaneously during a transition period — producing hybrid signatures that clients can verify with classical tools today and quantum-resistant tools later — without breaking the existing PKCS#11 interface? Success criterion: a hybrid signing command that passes interoperability tests with at least one external verifier.

3. **Side-channel exposure:** Does the ML-DSA implementation on the Cortex-M7, under the memory optimisations required by question 1, introduce measurable leakage through timing or power side channels that would be flagged in a Common Criteria EAL4+ evaluation? Success criterion: a documented test result using standard side-channel evaluation methodology, with any identified leakage points and proposed countermeasures.
