## Reducing Word-Error Rate for Serbian Child Speech in a Maths-Problem Reading Feature

**Company:** Prosveta Digital preduzetnik, Zrenjanin **Faculty:** Fakultet tehničkih nauka, Univerzitet u Novi Sad, Department of Power, Electronics and Telecommunications **Researcher:** Željko Uzelac (Жељко Узелац), viši naučni saradnik **Company contact:** Milena Ostojić, founder and product lead [C127-P1]

---

### The problem

Prosveta Digital's web portal includes a prototype speech-input feature where a child reads a maths problem aloud and the system checks whether they misread key numbers or skipped operation words such as 'minus' or 'podijeli'. Tests with eight children aged 8–10 using the Web Speech API and two open-source models produced word-error rates above 40% in every case, with consistent failures on Serbian number words above twenty and short function words [C127]. This matches the zero-shot WER range of 38.7%–61.4% that Whisper checkpoints produced on Serbian child speech benchmarks in quiet and classroom-noise conditions [SYN-PUB-01737]. The feature cannot be released at that accuracy.

---

### Research questions

1. Can a domain-adapted wav2vec 2.0 or fine-tuned Whisper model, trained on read child speech in Serbian and constrained with a narrow maths-vocabulary language model, bring WER below 15% on connected number-word and operation-word sequences spoken by children aged 8–10?
2. How many prompted adaptation utterances per child are needed before the system achieves reliable detection of digit-transposition errors (e.g. '47' read as '74') and omitted operation words, given that MLLR adaptation with ten utterances per child reached 28.7% WER and CMLLR reached 26.1% WER in an earlier isolated-word study? [SYN-PUB-01727]
3. Does a post-processing layer that maps ASR output to a structured semantic check (correct numbers present, correct operation word present) add measurable detection accuracy beyond raw WER improvements alone?

### What each side brings

**Prosveta Digital:** Access to children aged 8–10 in a natural maths-task context; an existing web portal with speech-input scaffolding [C127-P3]; Milena Ostojić's curriculum knowledge to define exactly which number and operation words must be recognised; roughly one day per week from Milena and four hours per week from the portal developer for integration and testing [C127].

**FTN / Željko Uzelac:** Domain expertise in acoustic model adaptation for Serbian child speech [SYN-PUB-01727]; a 120-hour annotated corpus of Serbian child speech aged 6–14 from project SYN-PRJ-0285 that can be used as pre-training data; benchmark methodology for comparing ASR back-ends under educational software conditions [SYN-PUB-01733]; GPU cluster and anechoic chamber for recording and model training [R0131]; prior collaboration with an educational software company on voice interface integration [R0131].

---

### Why this pair

Uzelac has directly measured the gap the company is hitting: zero-shot Whisper WER on Serbian child speech runs from 38.7% to 61.4% [SYN-PUB-01737], which matches Prosveta Digital's own 40%+ results [C127]. He has also shown that speaker adaptation can cut WER from 41.3% to 26.1% on Serbian child speech with a small number of utterances per child [SYN-PUB-01727], and he holds a corpus of 600 annotated child speakers that would otherwise take the company years to collect [SYN-PRJ-0285]. Prosveta Digital brings what Uzelac explicitly says he prefers in a partner: an active educational platform with access to child speech material and a concrete application context [R0131]. Milena Ostojić is also a co-author on the 2026 Whisper evaluation study [SYN-PUB-01737], which means both sides already share a working understanding of the problem. Note: Uzelac has moderate capacity for new projects until end of 2026 [R0131]; the project starts January 2027, when the EduVoice-RS-HU workload is expected to have wound down [SYN-PRJ-0286].
